      How the index is calculated

      Every important system contributes its business impact times how likely an attacker is to reach it along the easiest route. Three things set that likelihood.

      Business impactWhat the route reaches. Regulated personal data, money movement, and the systems that control everything else weigh most; test systems never count.
      Adversary likelihoodHow likely each step is to be walked: seen exploited in the wild, how much skill it takes, and whether the technique is used by the actors targeting this industry and region. The hardest step governs the route.
      Remaining defensive gapWhat is left after working controls are credited. A working firewall or strong sign-in shrinks the gap; a bypassed one, or one whose evidence is out of date, does not.
      index = sum over important systems of impact × likelihood of the easiest route likelihood = starting position × (threat × ease × remaining gap) for each step threat = discounted for actor relevance, by at most half — never for a step seen exploited in the wild

      Same number as the exposure score on the Executive tab, read as a balance: it accumulates when routes appear and is paid down when they are cut. Unknowns widen the range; they never shrink the debt.

      Where the debt comes from

      How much of the index would go if every weakness of that kind were fixed. One route can cross several kinds, so the rows overlap and do not add up to the total.

      What is holding the debt down

      How much higher the index would be without each control, counted only where its evidence is current.

      Break the route, retire the debt

      A route depends on a chain of weaknesses: a vulnerability here, a misconfiguration there, an identity gap bridging them. Cut one link that every route needs and the findings underneath leave the route — their deadlines set by policy or regulation still run. The plan on the Executive tab is ordered by exactly that.
